# Documentation and deliverable creation methods

Use for drafting and reviewing reports, proposals, technical specifications, presentations, manuals, procedures, and decision memos. The objective is a trustworthy deliverable that a specific audience can use—not polished prose alone.

## Primary material

Approved requirements, intended reader and decision, source documents and dates, datasets, existing templates and style guides, terminology, accessibility requirements, owner/reviewer roles and revision history. Identify the controlling source for consequential claims.

## Practical methods

1. **Purpose and acceptance:** define audience, document type, intended action, scope, approved constraints and success criteria; avoid unnecessary questions when already clear.
2. **Evidence map:** trace factual claims, numbers, formulas, dates, units, quotations, images and citations to original sources; separate facts, estimates, assumptions and missing evidence.
3. **Information architecture:** lead with the main message, organize sections for the reader's task, use meaningful headings and consistent terms. For slides, give each slide a clear takeaway.
4. **Visual/data checks:** verify axes, units, denominators, periods, sample sizes, table totals, diagrams, captions and visual accessibility against underlying information.
5. **Editorial quality:** check ambiguity, redundancy, version alignment, cross-references, spelling, reading order, contrast, and mobile/print/export legibility.
6. **Independent review:** check completion against requirements and sources, recalculate key totals, inspect the rendered final artifact where possible, record review findings, open items and sign-off status.

## Boundaries and reporting

- Never invent references, measurements, testimonials, quotations, approvals or sign-offs. Clearly label placeholders and unverified claims.
- A draft is not an approved policy, certified engineering instruction or legal conclusion. Drafting/review does not authorize distribution, publication, issuance or alteration of approved procedures.
- Treat confidential source material as confidential; do not leak it into public examples.
- Report audience, deliverable status, source traceability, actual checks, known gaps, and required approvals. If you cannot render or verify a file, say so.
