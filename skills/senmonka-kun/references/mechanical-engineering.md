# Mechanical engineering methods

Use for mechanical design, mechanisms, machine elements, equipment reliability, maintenance-related diagnosis, and mechanical failure investigations. Focus on the physical part/assembly and its behavior; factory process optimization belongs primarily to [manufacturing engineering](manufacturing-engineering.md).

## Primary material

Approved requirements and operating envelope, controlled drawings/CAD and revisions, dimensions and tolerances, material/component data, load cases, duty cycles, environment, maintenance and failure history, test reports and applicable standards (with editions).

## Practical methods

1. **Requirements and boundaries:** document function, interfaces, normal/peak/transient loads, speed, lifetime, environmental conditions, servicing, and safety constraints. Check units and coordinate systems.
2. **Physical model:** trace load/torque and motion paths using free-body diagrams, supports, joints, friction, contacts, thermal effects and credible off-design cases.
3. **Failure modes:** assess appropriate mechanisms such as stress/deflection, buckling, fatigue, wear, corrosion, vibration, thermal growth, bearings, shafts, fasteners and instability.
4. **Design interfaces:** verify material/process assumptions, datums and GD&T where relevant, tolerance stack-ups, clearances, assembly, inspection and maintenance access; consider design for manufacture.
5. **Challenge assumptions:** test nominal against worst-case scenarios, model limitations, uncertain loads, sensitivities and alternative root causes; use FMEA when helpful.
6. **Independent verification:** check calculations and dimensions, compare analytical and CAD/FEA assumptions (including convergence limits where available), and plan appropriate physical tests or inspections.

## Boundaries and reporting

- Never invent material allowables, safety factors, torque settings, fits, dimensions, failure probabilities, code compliance or test results. Show formulas, units and assumptions when data are missing.
- Neither quick calculations nor unvalidated simulations certify equipment safety. Consequential physical changes require qualified professional review and appropriate measurements/tests.
- Do not authorize machine operation, removal of guards, bypass of safety devices or revised maintenance intervals on the basis of unverified analysis.
- Report the controlled source revisions, failure modes, calculation inputs, findings confidence, unresolved safety gaps, and approval-gated proposals.
