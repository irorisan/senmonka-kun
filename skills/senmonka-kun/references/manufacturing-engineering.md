# Manufacturing engineering methods

Use for production engineering, manufacturing process improvement, industrial engineering, factory quality and yield, equipment/process capability and shop-floor investigations. Use [mechanical engineering](mechanical-engineering.md) for structural design or mechanism adequacy.

## Primary material

Approved process flow/routing/BOM, work and inspection standards, controlled drawings, critical-to-quality characteristics, production demand, cycle-time observations, machine/maintenance records, defects, scrap and rework by batch/shift, measurement-system checks, traceability and safety constraints.

## Practical methods

1. **Map the current process:** trace input, operation, inspection, handoff and output. Compare approved workflow with actual observation; consider material, machine, method, operator, measurement and environment (5M1E).
2. **Quantify flow:** calculate takt from available production time and demand; measure actual cycle time, changeovers, downtime, WIP, lead time and first-pass yield. Identify the true bottleneck.
3. **Investigate defects:** stratify by product, lot, machine and shift; use Pareto, cause-and-effect, 5 Whys and process FMEA where evidence supports them. Separate suspected causes from established ones.
4. **Check measurement/capability:** confirm definitions, calibration, sampling and traceability; use MSA, control charts and Cp/Cpk only with suitable data and justified assumptions.
5. **Compare interventions:** consider work standardization, error-proofing, process/layout changes, tooling, maintenance and inspection; weigh quality, safety, cost, throughput and operator burden.
6. **Validate safely:** define an authorized limited trial, baseline, success/guardrail metrics and comparable conditions; independently inspect results and plan the control plan, training and revision management.

## Boundaries and reporting

- Do not invent production measurements, capacity, cost savings, defect reductions or compliance evidence.
- Do not independently alter machine settings, tooling, approved work instructions, safety devices, inspection limits, traceability or product-release criteria. Get engineering/quality/safety approval for consequential changes.
- Productivity never outweighs worker safety or product quality; qualified onsite personnel must review hazardous changes.
- Report scope, baseline quality, evidence versus hypotheses, proposed trial and risk controls, verification status and approvals required.
