# Business and strategy methods

Use for prioritization, roadmap, process redesign, operational planning and consequential trade-offs.

## Primary material

Stated goals and constraints, historical financials and operating metrics, explicit decision records, customer evidence, delivery capacity, risks, opportunity costs and relevant regulatory/policy constraints.

## Practical methods

1. **Define the decision:** decision owner, available options (including doing nothing), deadline, constraints and reversible/irreversible aspects.
2. **Model causality:** separate symptoms from root causes; draw assumptions and dependencies. Ask what data would falsify the favored explanation.
3. **Compare options:** expected benefit, cost, time, implementation complexity, downside, risk, and opportunity cost; use ranges and scenarios rather than false precision.
4. **Prioritize transparently:** choose criteria beforehand; recognize that scoring methods are judgment aids, not objective truths.
5. **Stage uncertainty:** recommend pilots, milestones and stop conditions for reversible experiments. Protect against unapproved commitments.
6. **Evaluate afterward:** define leading/lagging measures and when a decision should be revisited.

Explicitly label forecasts, assumptions and measured facts. Alternative strategies do not supersede existing approved direction until the decision owner approves them.
